# 80/20 Arabic

**See the Qur’an. Understand the structure.**  
A Qur’an-first, 31-week learning product for students who are memorising the Qur’an and want to recognise the Arabic words and structures they already encounter.

- **App repository:** https://github.com/moosa198/80-20-Arabic

## What students can do

- Follow the **31-week sequence** across foundations, relationships, connected meaning and independent reading.
- Work through three **Al-Fātiḥah retrieval checkpoints** in weeks 8, 16 and 24.
- Learn seven lexical words in each teaching week. High-frequency grammatical/function words have their **own recurring box and review set**, rather than displacing the seven new lexical slots.
- Review in three separate modes: **Qur’an vocabulary**, **high-frequency Qur’an words**, and **grammar terms English ↔ Arabic**.
- See technical terms in paired form every time they are taught or retrieved, for example **Pronoun — ضمير**, **Nominal sentence — جملة اسمية**, **Preposition phrase — جار ومجرور**, and **Emphasis — توكيد**.
- Apply each idea to the indicated Madani 15-line Mushaf page, practise guided tarkīb (تركيب), and use FSTU Naḥw (نحو) and Ṣarf (صرف) for targeted follow-up.
- Build an independent habit by revisiting concepts on another page, with progressively less scaffolding.
- Track learning locally, export a progress backup, and import it on the same or another browser.

## Learning rhythm

Designed around a 45-minute weekly class, plus short practice sessions between classes:

1. **Retrieve** old learning before looking at notes.
2. **Recognise** seven new lexical words and the week’s high-value structure.
3. **Connect** the English grammar label with the Arabic term.
4. **Apply** it on the target Qur’an page and explain the Arabic evidence.
5. **Return after a gap** using short reviews instead of cramming.

A useful between-class rhythm is about 5 minutes of vocabulary recall, 10–15 minutes of targeted FSTU practice, and 5–10 minutes finding the same feature on a different memorised page. Adjust the dose for the age and capacity of the students. Accuracy and transfer matter more than speed on the first attempt.

## Learning design

The app uses active recall, spaced practice, mixed review, cumulative checkpoints and transfer tasks. The review scheduler uses simple self-ratings:
- **Again:** the answer was not recalled; return soon.
- **Hard:** recalled with difficulty; return sooner.
- **Good / Easy:** recall was stronger; increase the interval.

The “well-established” word count means a card has been answered successfully at least four times. It is a practical study signal, **not a formal measure of comprehension**. Independent understanding must still be checked by a teacher using fresh Qur’an passages and explanations backed by Arabic evidence. No fixed Qur’an comprehension percentage is promised from a word count alone.

## Privacy and offline use

The app is a static progressive web app. Course completion and review progress remain in the current browser's local storage; no account or server-side student profile is created. Use **Export progress** before changing devices. The service worker caches the app shell after the first online visit. Google Fonts may need an initial internet connection to load.

## Printable workbook and automated quality checks

The in-app **Print full course workbook** produces a student copy with writing space and no answer key. It includes a front section, two pages for each full teaching lesson, one-page checkpoints, a student-selected capstone vocabulary exercise, and progress trackers.

GitHub Actions uses `scripts/build-workbook.cjs` to test the actual rendered UI in Chromium before generating the PDF. Checks cover all 31 lessons, paired English/Arabic terminology, explicitly marked right-to-left Arabic, the seven-word pattern, Fātiḥah checkpoint behaviour, mobile width, grammar-term recall, and the expected page count. Screenshots and the content audit are stored as a workflow artifact.

## Maintain the bilingual terminology

The app's terminology map lives in `TERMS` in `index.html`. Store every technical label as an English/Arabic pair; render Arabic in a separate element with `lang="ar"` and `dir="rtl"`. Do not concatenate English and Arabic into one mixed-direction heading.

## Scope

This is a practical teaching aid, not a replacement for a qualified teacher, reliable tafsīr, dictionary work, or careful tarkīb. Vocabulary glosses are short recall cues rather than exhaustive lexical definitions. In the capstone, students choose words from their own memorised page and justify their analysis independently.
